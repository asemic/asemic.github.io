  <p style="line-height: 1.5;">
Good morning, Rosemarie,
<br>apologies for coming in without knocking,
<br>but it looks like you’re sleeping soundly.
<br>
<br>There you are on the table.
<br>In my head, I’m giving you the script:
<br>*Hi, I’m a medical student,
<br>What brings you in today,* but I know
<br>you aren’t speaking,
<br>you couldn’t, even if you tried.
<br>You don’t even know we’re here.
<br>
<br>Rosemarie – I’m sorry we had to meet under such circumstances.
<br>It’s not exactly inviting, is it?
<br>Formaldehyde. Fluorescent light.
<br>Us ten fresh-faced first-years
<br>staring speechless at your unmoving body.
<br>
<br>To be honest, I’m not listening to the lecturer.
<br>In front of me, you’re not dead, but warm, sunlit, eyes wide.
<br>I see your eighty summers in your tanned arms and sunburnt back,
<br>and suddenly I wonder if you cherished the body you lived in.
<br>
<br>I wonder if you hated it,
<br>whether the constant swelling of your legs
<br>made you wish for new ones,
<br>whether the scars from procedure after procedure
<br>made you hate doctors like us.
<br>
<br>I wonder if you were worried
<br>to place a pacemaker in your chest,
<br>whether you were relieved or unsettled
<br>by the presence of a cold, metal machine
<br>that close to your warm, beating heart.
<br>
<br>I place the cloth back over your face
<br>*How are you sleeping lately? Does pain ever wake you up at night?*
<br>I cut clumsy through layers of fat
<br>*How is your appetite? Three meals a day?*
<br>I slice the femoral artery in two,
<br>and I kill you a second time.
<br>
<br>Rosemarie – I’m sorry about that artery.
<br>I’m sorry that vessel can no longer propel you
<br>to crawl back into bed, wade into the river,
<br>or walk down the aisle.
<br>
<br>I’m sorry too that there is no blood to
<br>rush wild through your heart’s four chambers,
<br>as you run down the street with the other young girls,
<br>shrieking with childhood joy.
<br>
<br>I’m sorry that your knee can never again kneel
<br>to pick up your child, or to plant the flowers
<br>for which you were named.
<br>
<br>Rosemarie, there is so much more I want to ask you,
<br>but I fear I’ve already overstepped with my clamp and scalpel.
<br>I’ll leave you in peace with your answers, your stories.
<br>I hope you rest well.
</p>
